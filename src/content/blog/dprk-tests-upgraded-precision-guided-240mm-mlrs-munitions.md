North Korea has officially confirmed live-fire testing of upgraded precision-guided rockets for its 240mm Multiple Launch Rocket System (MLRS) conducted on September 22. This operational demonstration highlights Pyongyang’s accelerated transition from legacy, unguided mass-artillery saturation tactics toward modernized precision-strike doctrines. By expanding the operational footprint and accuracy of its heavy rocket artillery, the Korean People's Army (KPA) aims to hold high-value command nodes, air defense sites, and logistical hubs across the Korean Peninsula at risk with higher lethality and reduced munition expenditure.

The newly tested 240mm munitions integrate an autonomous high-precision guidance suite—likely combining inertial navigation with satellite guidance correction—to significantly reduce Circular Error Probable (CEP). According to technical telemetry released regarding the test, the system demonstrated an operational engagement range of 100 kilometers using a conventional ballistic trajectory, which extends to 115 kilometers when employing a combined aerodynamic glide-flight profile. This extended standoff envelope allows artillery batteries to operate out of range of standard counter-battery systems while maintaining deep-strike capabilities against critical rear-area targets.

From a strategic and logistical standpoint, the shift toward precision-guided 240mm artillery represents a cost-effective force multiplier that modernizes existing industrial production lines rather than requiring entirely new launcher chassis. Furthermore, the development of scalable, kit-based guidance solutions for standard artillery calibers presents strategic supply chain implications, particularly as North Korea continues to expand military-technical cooperation and arms transfers to foreign partners seeking high-volume, precision-guided tactical munitions.

In the short-term trajectory, KPA frontline artillery units along the Demilitarized Zone are expected to begin receiving these retrofitted or newly produced precision rounds to replace unguided inventories. Defense intelligence analysts expect follow-on trials focusing on multi-rocket volley accuracy, salvo dispersion under GPS-jamming conditions, and operational integration with forward spotter reconnaissance drones. The deployment of long-range, glide-capable MLRS rockets incrementally erodes traditional sanctuary zones, forcing allied forces to adapt their air defense coverage and counter-artillery targeting cycles.
